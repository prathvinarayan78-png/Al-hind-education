The six programmes

Each one small enough to check by hand, long enough to matter.

This page is the operating manual in public: how each programme works step by step, what a unit of it costs, and exactly where it needs hands. If a line here ever differs from the audited report, the report wins.

i.

Roshni Bridge Classes

Education · running since 2016

A Roshni centre is a ninety-minute evening class for children who are on a government school's register but read below their grade. Grouping is by reading level, not age. The class runs in space the village provides and ends — that is the design — when the child reads fluently and manages four-digit sums, at which point the school takes over.

How a centre happens, step by step:

  1. The panchayat or the school writes to the society inviting a centre.
  2. A cluster coordinator runs a baseline reading survey of the enrolled children — aloud, one by one, no group guessing.
  3. The village commits space and bench carpentry for two years; the society commits a trained teacher's honorarium, material and supervision.
  4. The teacher joins the June training; the centre opens after the monsoon.
  5. Every term, an unannounced visit; every child, a monthly reading check entered in the register.
  6. Exit: the child's name moves from our register to a "reading" column, and the school is informed in writing.

ii.

Kitab School-Kit Drive

Education · June, every year since 2018

The drive is timed to land before the term opens, because a kit in July is a kit and a kit in October is an apology. Each kit: canvas bag, three notebooks, slate and pencils, geometry box, steel bottle, two stitched uniforms.

The uniforms come off the Hunar centres' machines — the society's quietest circular economy. Distribution is at the school gate in school hours, reconciled line by line against the enrolment register. There is no ceremony and no photograph of a child holding the kit; the photographs of the kits are taken before the children arrive, which is why the gallery looks the way it does.

Cost per kit has been held at ₹1,080 for three years by buying at Sadar Bazaar wholesale and stitching in-house. Donors may sponsor kits in multiples of one; the receipt book does not care about round numbers.

iii.

Hunar Women's Skill Centres

Livelihood & education · since 2021

Six treadle machines, three hours a day, nine months, and a creche corner — because the difference between a course and a promise is usually a small child with nowhere to sit. The syllabus is seam to saree fall: stitching, finishing, cutting to pattern, and the arithmetic of a tailoring bill.

Graduates receive machine time and a starter toolkit; those who want it get preparation for the NIOS Class 8 or Class 10 open examination, and several have taken it. A graduate is counted placed only after she reports three consecutive months of income from her own stitching. Last year 61% did. We publish the rest of the number too.

The centres also stitch the Kitab uniforms and take paid alteration work from the bazaar, so the better batches earn while they learn — and learn while they earn.

iv.

Sehat Health Camps

Public welfare · since 2017, register format since 2021

A camp day runs on volunteer doctors and the village's own ASHA worker, whose register keeps the follow-up. Screening: blood pressure, blood sugar, haemoglobin, weight, eyes. What needs the community health centre gets a referral slip; seventy-one percent of last year's referrals were followed through, because the register lives in the village and the ASHA worker reads it out at the anganwadi.

Spectacles prescribed at camp are delivered fitted, free, at the next visit. We do not run a pharmacy, and we do not carry prescriptions from anyone who was not physically at the camp. Medicine without a doctor present is not medicine; it is risk with a label.

v.

Paani Water & Sanitation

Public welfare · since 2018

Repairs first: the society stocks rods, seals and chains, and trains two caretakers per pump, because a handpump without a caretaker is a future repair. Every repaired pump gets a soak pit so the water doesn't pool, and the panchayat adopts the pump by resolution — the adoption is the sustainability.

Second, the last fifteen percent: toilets that a scheme started and abandoned get the missing door, pan or ring beam, with the panchayat supplying the mason. 117 finished this way. Third, only when the first two are done in a village, new soak pits and drainage where the pump water runs toward a doorway.

vi.

Raahat Relief, Without Photographs

Public welfare · seasonal

Blankets in December and January; ration kits when the monsoon closes roads; dates and rations through Ramzan; qurbani meat on Eid al-Adha. Lists are drawn with the panchayat and the anganwadi worker, read out at the bhavan, and corrected in public before a single kit moves. Distribution is door-to-door.

This is the programme the society refuses to photograph, and the only one where we will not tell you heartwarming stories about named families. What we will show you is the signed list, the panchayat seal, and the accounts line. Relief that needs an audience is theatre; relief that needs a receipt is work.
